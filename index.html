When should we protect rate, and when should we buy occupancy?

August is the commercial peak in both volume and ADR. January is the baseline. Cancel rate rises with price through summer, so peak weeks need different rules than winter.

July–August (protect): Highest ADR and highest wash. Apply minimum length of stay and deposits on long-lead Online TA. Source: Data Analysis 3, confirmed in the file.
April–June / September (watch): ADR already high and cancel ~24–30%. Start the 7–14 day confirmation filter before peak.
November–January (fill): Lowest cancel (~21–22%) and lowest ADR. Flexible terms and Direct offers are safer here than in August.

Which channels and guest types produce stays we can keep?

Online TA is 59.2% of bookings and 35.5% cancel. Direct has almost the same ADR (~$118) with 14.8% cancel. Couples dominate volume; families are fewer but more expensive and more likely to cancel.

Which booking behaviors predict that the guest will actually arrive?

Lead time, parking, special requests, modifications, and room assignment separate arrivals better than “couple vs family.” Use these as a scoring list for overbooking and pre-arrival outreach.

Parking: 8.4% of bookings request a space and none of those rows are canceled in this file. Use as a score and a booking prompt; it is association, not a proven causal lever.
Modifications: 18.1% change the booking and cancel at 15.6% vs 30.3% with zero changes.
Non Refund rows: only 1,037 bookings, and 94.7% are flagged canceled. That is prepaid forfeiture in this schema, not “the guest still came.” Do not read Non Refund as a low-cancel product.
